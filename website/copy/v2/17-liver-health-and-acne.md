# Liver Health and Acne: How Your Body Clears Excess Estrogen

Your body clears used estrogen every day: your liver tags it for removal, and it leaves through your gut and urine. No cleanse can "flush out" excess estrogen faster, but less alcohol, more fibre and a regular gut keep clearance from slowing.

This page covers everyday liver function, not liver disease. If you're worried about your liver itself, go straight to [when to see a doctor](#see-a-doctor).

## Your liver clears estrogen in three steps {#three-steps}

Your liver clears estrogen in three steps: it changes estrogen's shape, tags it for removal, and sends it out through bile and urine.

What this means for acne is narrower than many sites suggest. Most pre-period breakouts follow normal cycle shifts. When estrogen runs high compared with progesterone, breakouts can follow. No study has tested whether faster clearance leads to clearer skin.

If your breakouts come with tender breasts and heavier periods, read about the [signs of high estrogen and estrogen dominance](/blogs/learn/estrogen-dominance-and-acne).

### The liver changes estrogen's shape (phase 1)

The liver changes estrogen's shape in phase 1. Enzymes turn estrogen into one of several breakdown products. Some of these forms are more reactive, so they need to move on to phase 2.

### The liver tags it for removal (phase 2)

The liver tags estrogen for removal in phase 2. Three main reactions do the tagging: glucuronidation, sulfation and methylation. Each attaches a molecule that makes estrogen water-soluble, so it can leave in bile or urine. Glutathione, which your liver makes, handles some of the more reactive forms.

### The gut carries it out, or sends some back

The gut carries tagged estrogen out in your stool, or sends some back. Gut bacteria make an enzyme called beta-glucuronidase that can remove the tag. Once untagged, some estrogen is reabsorbed into your blood. Researchers call the gut bacteria that handle estrogen the estrobolome.

A [2025 study of estrogens in stool and blood](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13006107) measured this in 30 healthy adults. Stool estrogens were higher in premenopausal women and rose across the cycle. The bacterial genes for beta-glucuronidase and a related enzyme, arylsulfatase, tracked with some blood estrogens. The authors call this partial support for gut recycling, not proof.

Calcium D-glucarate is an ingredient studied for its effect on beta-glucuronidase. Here's [what calcium D-glucarate does](/blogs/learn/calcium-d-glucarate). <!-- plain text until February 2027 --> Three things slow this exit: alcohol, low fibre and constipation.

> **Your liver also affects testosterone.** Your liver makes sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG), which binds testosterone. High insulin means less SHBG, which leaves more free testosterone to reach your oil glands.

If your breakouts started after you stopped the pill, androgens are the likelier driver, so read [how high testosterone affects your skin](/blogs/learn/high-testosterone-and-acne).

## Does alcohol cause acne? {#alcohol}

Alcohol isn't an established direct cause of acne, but it gives your liver more to clear, and women's livers are more vulnerable to it.

Women are more susceptible to alcohol-related liver injury than men, according to the [American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases](https://www.aasld.org/practice-guidelines/alcohol-associated-liver-disease). [CHECK: confirm the AASLD page states this; our tool couldn't verify the wording] Alcohol also reduces your body's ability to break down estrogen, according to [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/22363-high-estrogen). Some women notice they handle alcohol less well in their 40s, during perimenopause.

Small changes are easier to keep than an all-or-nothing rule. Try these:

- Pick 2 or 3 alcohol-free days a week.
- Pour smaller. A standard glass of wine is about 5 oz (150 mL), according to the [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/alcohol/standard-drink-sizes/index.html).
- Swap in sparkling water with citrus on social nights.

[Canada's Guidance on Alcohol and Health](https://www.ccsa.ca/canadas-guidance-alcohol-and-health) says 2 drinks or fewer a week keeps risk low. The current US Dietary Guidelines no longer give a daily number; they say to drink less alcohol for better health.

Judge your skin over about 3 cycles (about 3 months) after cutting back, because skin changes show slowly. Some women break out without drinking at all, so alcohol is one factor, not the whole story.

## Fibre and a regular gut keep estrogen moving out {#fibre}

Fibre and a regular gut keep estrogen moving out: the longer stool sits, the more time gut bacteria have to send some estrogen back.

If you've searched how to lower estrogen naturally, the better goal is keeping clearance moving, not pushing estrogen down. Estrogen supports bone health, according to the [Endocrine Society](https://www.endocrine.org/patient-engagement/endocrine-library/menopause-and-bone-loss), and it supports your skin too.

These four habits keep clearance moving, each with one easy swap.

1. **Fibre at every meal.** Beans, lentils, oats, berries and chia all count; flax is optional. Swap: add a spoon of chia or a handful of berries to your breakfast.
2. **Regular digestion.** Aim for a daily bowel movement. Swap: drink a glass of water with each meal.
3. **Cruciferous vegetables.** Broccoli, cauliflower, kale and cabbage. Swap: add a side of greens to dinner.
4. **Protein at each meal.** Phase 2 needs amino acids from protein. Swap: add eggs, yogurt, fish or tofu to the meal that's lightest on protein.

[IMAGE: Broccoli, lentils and oats on a kitchen counter. Alt text: "Broccoli, lentils and oats, everyday high-fibre foods".]

Add fibre slowly to avoid bloating and gas while your gut adjusts. Fibre helps keep bowel movements regular, and high-fibre foods support a diverse mix of gut bacteria. If constipation lasts, tell your doctor.

Probiotics for acne are unsettled: a few small trials look promising, but none is large enough to rely on. Food triggers matter for many women too, so read about [sugar, dairy and the hormonal acne diet](/blogs/learn/hormonal-acne-diet). <!-- switch on March 2027 -->

No food clears estrogen on its own, but fibre at every meal gives your gut fewer chances to send it back.

## Your liver doesn't need a special program to clear estrogen {#cleanse}

You don't need a liver cleanse: your liver already clears hormones every day, and no cleanse kit has good evidence that it helps.

If you've searched for a liver detox, here's the short answer. [Johns Hopkins Medicine](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/health/expert-qa/detoxing-your-liver-fact-versus-fiction) says cleanse products haven't been proven to reverse liver damage, and some supplements can harm the liver. NCCIH finds even milk thistle's liver evidence conflicting. The habits that support your liver are the ones above: less alcohol, more fibre, protein and a regular gut.

At Cocoon, we don't explain new breakouts away. The table sets out the claims we hear most and what the evidence says.

| A "sluggish liver" causes acne | "Sluggish liver" isn't a diagnosis, and it isn't an established cause of acne |

| You need a cleanse a few times a year | Your liver works every day; no cleanse kit has good evidence |
| Natural means safe | Some herbal blends and concentrated extracts are linked to liver injury |
| More herbs mean more support | Each extra herb adds side effects and interactions, not proven benefit |

> **When to stop.** Some women notice a short adjustment period when they start a new supplement. Stop and contact Cocoon if you get new or deeper cysts in new areas or a rash. Do the same if your skin is still worse after 6 to 8 weeks.

Your liver doesn't need a cleanse. It needs fewer obstacles: less alcohol, more fibre and a regular gut.

## When should you see a doctor about your liver? {#see-a-doctor}

See a doctor about your liver if your skin or eyes turn yellow, or you itch all over with no rash. Acne isn't one of the warning signs.

> **See a doctor if you notice:**
> - Yellowing skin or eyes
> - Itching with no rash
> - Spider-like blood vessels on your face, chest or shoulders
> - Dark urine or pale stools
> - Swelling in your legs or belly
> - Discomfort under your right ribs
> - Ongoing tiredness, nausea or loss of appetite
>
> **Yellowing eyes, new belly swelling, confusion or significant belly pain, each on its own, means get medical care promptly.**

[Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/17179-liver-disease) lists most of these as later signs of liver disease. Your doctor may order a liver blood panel: ALT, AST, ALP, GGT, bilirubin and albumin. If you drink most days or take daily medicines, ask about these tests before you try liver herbs.

## Questions women ask about the liver and acne {#faq}

### How long does it take your body to clear excess estrogen?

There's no finish line, because your body clears estrogen all the time. After cutting alcohol or adding fibre, judge your skin over about 3 cycles (about 3 months). Changes show slowly.

### Can liver problems cause acne?

Acne isn't a recognized sign of liver disease, and "liver acne" isn't a diagnosis. See a doctor for yellowing skin or eyes, itching with no rash, dark urine or pale stools.

### Do you need both a DIM supplement and a liver supplement?

No. The 2024 JCAD review found no acne trials of DIM, and liver supplements have no good acne evidence. Start with one over about 3 cycles, and ask your doctor or pharmacist before combining.

### What's the best drink for your liver?

There's no special drink; drinking less alcohol matters most. Water doesn't speed up your liver. Liver teas are traditional, with little human research. [CHECK: the brief's answer also says regular coffee is linked with better liver health; add once a source is found]

### Are milk thistle, dandelion and burdock safe to take?

Milk thistle is usually well tolerated; less is known about the other two. Take care with daisy-family allergies, and with dandelion if you take diuretics or lithium. Skip all three in pregnancy or breastfeeding. [NEED: naturopath confirms]

### Can a liver supplement make you break out at first?

Some people report it, but it isn't toxins leaving. Stop if you get new or deeper cysts in new areas, a rash, or skin still worse after 6 to 8 weeks. Tell your doctor, or Cocoon if it's ours.

### Does supporting your liver help hormonal acne?

There's no good clinical evidence that liver supplements clear hormonal acne. Your liver clears estrogen daily, and less alcohol and a regular gut support that. Judge any supplement over about 3 cycles.

### What vitamins or supplements are good for your liver?

Milk thistle has the most research, and NCCIH calls it conflicting. Most other liver supplements have little human data. If you're worried about your liver, see a doctor before buying anything. [NEED: naturopath to confirm]

### What are the warning signs of liver damage?

Yellowing skin or eyes, dark urine, pale stools, itching with no rash, and swelling in your legs or belly. See the full list in the doctor section above. Don't self-treat these with supplements. [NEED: naturopath to confirm the list]

### What are the side effects of burdock root, and is it safe every day?

Burdock has only small human studies, so its daily safety isn't well known. Avoid it if you're pregnant or allergic to daisy-family plants. [NEED: naturopath to confirm side effects and daily use]

### Does the liver regulate hormones?

The liver breaks down and clears many hormones, not only estrogen. It also makes SHBG, which carries sex hormones in your blood. No supplement has been shown to change that role in a way that affects skin.

### What foods are good for the liver?

Cruciferous vegetables, beans, lentils, oats, berries, and protein foods such as fish, eggs and tofu all fit the habits above. The evidence comes mostly from general healthy-eating research rather than liver-specific trials. [NEED: naturopath to confirm]
