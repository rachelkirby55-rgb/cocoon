## Choosing a formula: start with your breakout pattern {#choosing}

Choosing a formula starts with when and where you break out. Estrogen Balance is for breakouts linked to high estrogen; Androgen Balance (January 2027) is for breakouts linked to high testosterone and other androgens.

### Which Cocoon formula is right for me?

It depends on your pattern. Breakouts before your period, with tender breasts or bloating, fit the high-estrogen pattern and Estrogen Balance. Breakouts on the jaw, chin or body, with oily skin, fit the high-testosterone pattern and Androgen Balance. [The hormonal acne quiz](/pages/quiz) suggests a likely pattern, not a diagnosis.

### Can I buy Androgen Balance or Liver Support yet?

Not yet. Androgen Balance launches in January 2027 and Liver Support in April 2027, and you can join the waitlist for both now. Estrogen Balance is available to order today.

### Can I take Estrogen Balance and Androgen Balance together?

For now, start with one formula. The two share no active ingredients, but we haven't confirmed combined use yet. If you're considering both later, check both labels with your pharmacist. [NEED: formulator's advice on combined use]

### Can I add Liver Support to Estrogen Balance or Androgen Balance?

For now, start with your core formula on its own. Liver Support is designed as an optional add-on, and we'll publish our naturopath's guidance before it launches in April 2027. Milk thistle and berberine both carry blood-sugar cautions, so ask your pharmacist before combining them. [NEED: formulator confirms add-on use and the naturopath's line on milk thistle with berberine]

### Can I take Cocoon with other supplements, like zinc, spearmint or inositol?

Add one new supplement at a time, and check with your pharmacist first. Count all your zinc together: the NIH sets the adult upper limit at 40 mg a day. Androgen Balance will contain 20 mg of zinc a day, so leave room for it.

### Can men take Estrogen Balance?

Yes, men 18 and over can take Estrogen Balance. Check with your doctor first. [NEED: your call to keep or drop this question]

### Is Androgen Balance made for a medical condition?

No. Androgen Balance is for breakouts linked to high testosterone. If you have PCOS (now also called PMOS) or take blood-sugar medicine, talk to your doctor before you start. [NEED: compliance review; optional question]

### Is Cocoon safe to take with my medications?

It depends on the medicine. Find your formula's column in the table above and go through it with your doctor or pharmacist before you start. Don't take any Cocoon formula if you're pregnant, breastfeeding or under 18.

### Can I take DIM while on birth control?

Check with your doctor or pharmacist first: research can't yet say whether DIM affects how the pill works. Keep taking your pill as prescribed. St. John's wort is the best-known supplement that affects the pill, and none of our formulas contain it. [NEED: naturopath confirms where each Cocoon ingredient stands with the pill]

### Can I take Estrogen Balance with a hormonal IUD like Mirena or Kyleena?

Check with your doctor before you start. There are two questions to ask: whether DIM could affect your IUD, and whether your IUD could change how Estrogen Balance works for you. [NEED: naturopath's line on both]

### Can I take Cocoon with spironolactone, or use it to come off spironolactone?

Never start, stop or change spironolactone without your prescriber. Cocoon isn't a replacement for a prescription. If you want to come off spironolactone, plan it with your prescriber first. Read [spironolactone and supplements, what's been studied](/blogs/learn/spironolactone-and-supplements). [Developer: switch this link on in February, when the article goes live]

### I'm coming off the pill. Should I start before or after I stop?

[NEED: naturopath's timing.] Talk to your prescriber before you stop. Breakouts often start a few months after stopping the pill, not right away. Give your skin time to show its pattern before you choose a formula.

### Is it safe with an antidepressant like Lexapro or Zoloft?

Check with your pharmacist first. Estrogen Balance contains no 5-HTP, St. John's wort, SAMe or L-tryptophan, the supplements best known for affecting antidepressants. DIM may affect liver enzymes, which can change how some medicines are processed.

### Can I take it with HRT, an estrogen patch or testosterone?

Not without your prescriber's OK. DIM supports estrogen metabolism, so it may work against estrogen therapy. [NEED: naturopath confirms] The same caution applies to testosterone therapy.

### Can I take it with thyroid medicine like levothyroxine?

Ask your pharmacist first. [NEED: naturopath's line on Estrogen Balance with thyroid medicine, and on DIM and the thyroid itself] We don't make any claim about Cocoon and your thyroid, either way.

### I've had breast cancer, or I take tamoxifen or an aromatase inhibitor. Can I take DIM?

Don't take Estrogen Balance unless your oncologist says it's OK. DIM can affect how your body processes estrogen. Memorial Sloan Kettering lists cautions for DIM alongside these medicines.

### Can I take acne supplements with Accutane (isotretinoin)?

Ask your dermatologist first. No Cocoon formula contains vitamin A, based on each label. If you use tretinoin, adapalene (as in Epiduo) or benzoyl peroxide on your skin, ask your dermatologist too. [NEED: naturopath confirms the topicals line]

### Can I take Androgen Balance with birth control, metformin or a GLP-1?

Ask your doctor or pharmacist before you start, once it launches in January 2027. Berberine needs a check if you take blood-sugar medicines such as metformin or a GLP-1. Saw palmetto needs a check with birth control and blood thinners. NAC needs a check with asthma medicines or before surgery.

### Does the milk thistle in Liver Support interfere with birth control?

Ask your pharmacist before you start, once it launches in April 2027. [NEED: naturopath's line on milk thistle and the pill] Check too if you're allergic to daisy-family plants, or take blood-sugar medicine, a diuretic or lithium.

## Pregnancy, breastfeeding and under 18: don't take Cocoon {#pregnancy}

Pregnancy, breastfeeding and being under 18 are the times not to take any Cocoon formula. If you're trying to conceive, stop [NEED: naturopath's timing] before you start trying, and talk to your doctor.

**Don't take Cocoon if you're:**
- Pregnant or trying to conceive
- Breastfeeding
- Under 18

### Can I take Cocoon while pregnant or trying to conceive?

No. Don't take any Cocoon formula while you're pregnant. If you're trying to conceive, stop [NEED: naturopath's timing] before you start trying, and talk to your doctor about your skin.

### Can I take Cocoon while breastfeeding?

No. Don't take any Cocoon formula while you're breastfeeding. Our formulas are for after you've finished. In the meantime, talk to your doctor about your breakouts.

### Will my acne come back when I stop to try for a baby?

It can. Breakouts often return when you stop what was working. Stop [NEED: naturopath's timing] before you start trying, and ask your doctor about skincare that's suitable in pregnancy.

### Can teenagers take DIM for acne?

We don't recommend Estrogen Balance or our other formulas for anyone under 18. Teens should start with a family doctor or a dermatologist.

## The first weeks: know the stop rule, then judge it at about 3 cycles {#first-weeks}

The first weeks can bring mild, short-lived changes. Stop and contact us if you get new or deeper cysts in new areas, a rash, or skin still worse after 6-8 weeks.

| | |
|---|---|
| **What's normal** | Some people notice mild changes in the first week or two. |
| **Stop and contact us** | New or deeper cysts in new areas, a rash, or skin that's still worse after 6-8 weeks. |
| **Judge it at about 3 cycles** | About 3 months, then a check-in with us to decide whether to taper or continue. |

A worse start isn't a sign it's working. Breakouts can return when you stop.

### What might I notice in the first weeks?

Some people notice a mild headache, an upset stomach or a change in urine colour in the first 1-2 weeks. [NEED: naturopath confirms this list] Taking your capsule with food can help.

### My skin is worse since I started. Should I stop?

Stop and contact us if you get new or deeper cysts in new areas, a rash, or skin that's still worse after 6-8 weeks. Otherwise, contact us anyway and we'll look at it with you. Here's how we think about [telling an early flare from a reaction](/blogs/learn/skin-purging-vs-breakout).

### Can Androgen Balance make me break out at first?

It can. Some people break out when they start berberine or NAC. The same stop rule applies: new or deeper cysts in new areas, a rash, or skin still worse after 6-8 weeks. Androgen Balance launches in January 2027, so there are no reviews or results yet.

### How long until I see a difference?

Give it about 3 cycles, or about 3 months, before you judge it. There are no acne trials of DIM, so this timeline comes from our customers. Most Estrogen Balance reviewers who mention timing noticed a first change at about 3-6 weeks. [NEED: verify against the review recount]

### Do I have to take it forever?

No. Take it daily for about 3 cycles, then check in with us to decide whether to taper or continue. Breakouts can return when you stop, so we'll plan the next step with you. Androgen Balance's label asks you to check with a practitioner for use beyond 3 months.

### Is it safe to take Estrogen Balance long term?

[NEED: naturopath's position first.] Then, if confirmed: once your skin settles, some customers move to a maintenance dose of 1 capsule every other day. Long-term research on DIM is limited, so check in with your doctor if you plan to take it for many months.

### When should I take it, and what if I miss a dose?

Take 1 Estrogen Balance capsule a day with food, at about the same time each day. If you miss a dose, carry on the next day. Don't double up.

### Do I need to change my diet while I take it?

No special diet is needed. Food can affect breakouts, though. If you want to test your triggers, try cutting dairy (especially whey) or added sugar, one at a time.
